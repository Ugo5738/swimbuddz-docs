# Modular Academy Curriculum and Stackable Pathways

_Status: product and curriculum decision record_

_Last updated: July 2026_

## Purpose

This document answers whether the current 12-week Beginner-to-50m Freestyle
programme should be broken into reusable parts such as Intro to Water,
breathing, floating, propulsion, and stroke modules.

## Recommendation

Yes, make the curriculum modular, but do not turn the Academy into an
unrestricted list of unrelated short courses.

Use a **competency-gated, stackable model**:

1. An assessment establishes what a learner can already do.
2. Foundation modules build water safety, confidence, breathing, floating, and
   recovery.
3. Stroke modules use those foundation competencies as prerequisites.
4. Pathways bundle modules into easy-to-buy outcomes such as Beginner-to-50m
   Freestyle.
5. A learner may skip a prerequisite only when an assessment grants credit for
   it.

The programme remains structured while its parts become reusable.

## Important Distinction

### Free Intro-to-Water Assessment

The assessment is a short diagnostic and lead-generation activity. It is not a
free swimming lesson and does not promise that the learner will acquire a
skill.

It should produce:

- water-confidence and safety observations;
- demonstrated competency credits;
- risks or support needs;
- a recommended starting module;
- a recommended pathway and schedule;
- a follow-up offer within 24 hours.

### Water Confidence and Safety Module

This is a paid course. It teaches and verifies foundational competencies over
multiple coached sessions.

A prospect may therefore take the free assessment and be placed directly into
Breaststroke Foundation if they demonstrate all required foundation skills. A
true beginner would first complete the paid foundation modules.

## Proposed Competency Sequence

The final names can be made more customer-friendly, but the dependency order
should remain explicit.

| Code | Module | Typical length | Core outcome | Prerequisite |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| F1 | Water Confidence and Safety | 3-4 sessions | Safe entry/exit, submersion, calm recovery, pool rules | Assessment |
| F2 | Breathing and Bubble Control | 2-3 sessions | Controlled exhalation, rhythmic breathing preparation | F1 or assessed credit |
| F3 | Floating, Recovery and Body Position | 3-4 sessions | Front/back float, stand recovery, alignment, glide | F1 and F2 |
| F4 | Balance and Propulsion | 3-4 sessions | Streamline, kick, scull, short assisted travel | F2 and F3 |
| S1 | Freestyle Foundation | 4-6 sessions | Coordinated freestyle over a defined distance | F3 and F4 |
| S2 | Breaststroke Foundation | 4-6 sessions | Legal basic timing and coordinated breaststroke | F3 and F4 |
| S3 | Backstroke Foundation | 4-6 sessions | Stable back position, kick, rotation and arms | F3 and F4 |
| S4 | Butterfly Preparation | 4-6 sessions | Dolphin action and timing foundations | F4 plus stroke readiness |
| A1 | Deep Water Confidence | 3-4 sessions | Safe deep-water entry, treading, recovery and exit | F1-F3 |
| A2 | Distance and Independence | 4-6 sessions | Continuous independent swimming and pacing | At least one stroke module |

Session counts are planning ranges, not automatic passes. Completion is based
on demonstrated competencies, with a controlled make-up or extension window.

## Example Products

### Beginner-to-50m Freestyle Pathway

This remains the default beginner offer. It packages F1-F4, S1, and the
relevant parts of A1/A2 into one guided journey. Existing 12-week marketing can
continue where the local delivery plan supports it.

### Breaststroke Pathway

- New swimmer: assessment, F1-F4, then S2.
- Foundation graduate: S2 only.
- Existing swimmer with verified skills: assessment credit, then S2.

### Water-Safe Starter

F1-F3 as a lower-commitment product for someone whose immediate goal is water
confidence and safe recovery rather than a 50m stroke outcome.

### Multi-Stroke Bundle

Foundation credit plus two selected stroke modules. This should be sold as a
curated bundle, not as an unguided pick-any-two offer.

## Scheduling Model

Do not require every module to start every month at every location. That would
fragment demand and create small, uneconomic classes.

Recommended cadence:

| Activity | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Free assessment clinic | Monthly in active locations; 4-6 weeks before major intakes |
| F1 Water Confidence and Safety | Monthly where minimum demand is met |
| F2-F4 foundation continuation | Rolling from the preceding foundation group |
| Freestyle Foundation | At least quarterly, with extra starts when demand supports it |
| Breaststroke/Backstroke modules | Alternating or demand-triggered by location |
| Full beginner pathway | Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct anchor cohorts |
| Private or premium pathway | Scheduled against coach and pool capacity |

The quarterly anchor cohorts provide operational rhythm. Monthly foundation
starts create more entry points without forcing the entire Academy into
continuous enrollment.

## Cohort Design

A cohort is still a real social and delivery group. A learner should not move
between classes every week merely because modules are reusable.

Recommended rules:

- enrol learners into a scheduled module cohort;
- preserve the same coach and group where practical;
- record module credits on the learner, not only the cohort;
- allow progression at a module boundary, not in the middle of a session plan;
- reserve the three-week extension window for approved make-ups or competency
  completion;
- do not hold an entire cohort indefinitely for one learner;
- issue both module completion records and pathway certificates.

## Advantages

### Better placement

Learners stop repeating content they have already mastered. Assessment has a
clear operational purpose because it can grant prerequisite credit.

### More entry points

A learner who misses a January intake may take a monthly foundation module and
join a later stroke module instead of waiting a full quarter.

### Reusable curriculum

The same breathing, recovery, body-position, and safety competencies support
freestyle, breaststroke, backstroke, private lessons, and future programmes.

### Clearer recovery and make-ups

When absence affects one competency, the Academy can prescribe a specific
clinic or module extension rather than repeating a 12-week programme.

### Global consistency

Locations can use the same competency definitions even when cohort dates,
pools, languages, and weekly schedules differ.

### More precise sales

The assessment can recommend a concrete next step: “Breaststroke Foundation”
or “Water-Safe Starter,” not the vague choice between doing nothing and buying
12 weeks.

### Better lifetime value

Completion naturally leads to another stroke, distance development, or Club
practice. The pathway makes progression visible without creating artificial
course repetition.

## Disadvantages and Controls

### Scheduling fragmentation

Too many simultaneous modules can spread learners across uneconomic class
sizes.

**Control:** publish a controlled location timetable and open extra classes only
after a demand threshold or waitlist is reached.

### Choice overload

Prospects may not know whether they need breathing, floating, or breaststroke.

**Control:** sell outcomes and pathways first. Let assessment and the Academy
team prescribe modules.

### Uneven readiness

A learner may pass one skill in isolation but still lack integrated confidence.

**Control:** use observable competency rubrics, safety gates, and coach
judgement. A checkbox alone must not override a safety concern.

### Cohort disruption

Frequent entry and exit can weaken peer relationships and coach planning.

**Control:** intake at module boundaries and keep a stable group through a
module.

### Operational complexity

Prerequisites, credits, schedules, transfers, partial payments, and
certificates require better administration than one fixed programme.

**Control:** implement a module graph and competency ledger before offering
large-scale self-service module selection.

### Price comparison risk

A short module may look cheap while the complete pathway appears more
expensive than the former 12-week programme.

**Control:** show the complete recommended pathway price, offer bundle value,
and make credit valid so learners do not pay twice for passed modules.

### Incomplete learners

Some customers may buy only the enjoyable stroke part and avoid safety work.

**Control:** enforce prerequisites server-side and operationally. Stroke
enrollment is not available without foundation credit or assessment waiver.

## Is It Easier to Sell?

It is easier to sell when SwimBuddz sells a clear outcome and uses modules
behind the scenes. It is harder to sell when the prospect is shown a catalogue
of technical components and asked to assemble a course.

Recommended sales hierarchy:

1. **Primary:** outcome-based pathway, such as Beginner-to-50m Freestyle.
2. **After assessment:** prescribed starting module and total pathway.
3. **For returning learners:** next stroke or distance module based on existing
   credits.
4. **For price-sensitive prospects:** a meaningful starter outcome, not a
   random isolated lesson.

Every offer should show:

- who it is for;
- prerequisite or included assessment;
- observable completion outcome;
- expected sessions and extension policy;
- location and schedule;
- pathway price and module-only price;
- what credit the learner keeps after completion.

## Data and Product Model

The existing versioned curriculum, weeks, lessons, skills, and milestones are a
strong base. Modular delivery additionally needs:

| Concept | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `academy_modules` | Stable reusable learning unit |
| `module_versions` | Versioned curriculum and assessment rubric |
| `module_prerequisites` | Directed competency/module dependency graph |
| `program_module_composition` | Modules and ordering included in a pathway |
| `module_cohorts` | Scheduled location-specific delivery instance |
| `learner_module_credits` | Passed, assessed, waived, expired, or in-progress credit |
| `module_enrollments` | Enrollment and payment for a module cohort |
| `assessment_recommendations` | Evidence-backed placement result |

Do not duplicate the same lessons into every programme. A programme should
compose versioned modules, and a learner's credit should reference the exact
version or competency standard passed.

## Commercial Rules

- Bundle pricing does not have to equal the sum of standalone modules.
- A learner who already has verified foundation credit should not be charged
  again for those modules.
- Credit transfer between locations should be allowed when both locations use
  the same module version and safety standard.
- Pool, coach, and operating costs should be quoted for each module cohort.
- Private and premium-location cohorts may have separate prices.
- Club access begins after the learner completes the eligible pathway or
  graduation gate, not automatically after every short clinic.
- The one-month free Club bridge needs an explicit eligibility rule so a
  two-session clinic does not unintentionally grant the same benefit as a full
  pathway.

## Recommended Pilot

Pilot in one location before changing every Academy offer:

1. Map the current Beginner-to-50m curriculum into F1-F4 and S1.
2. Define pass criteria and safety gates for every competency.
3. Run monthly assessments and one monthly F1 start.
4. Keep the full 12-week pathway as the main product.
5. Offer Breaststroke Foundation only to assessed or foundation-credited
   learners.
6. Track class fill, completion, coach admin time, learner confusion, time to
   enrollment, and pathway revenue.
7. Review after two quarterly cycles before global rollout.

## Decision Summary

The modular model is doable and strategically stronger, but the product should
feel simpler to the learner than the underlying curriculum graph. SwimBuddz
should own the sequence, use assessment to personalize the entry point, and
retain clear pathway bundles as the main thing customers buy.

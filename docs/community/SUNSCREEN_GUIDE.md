## Why this needs its own guide

Generic sunscreen advice doesn't fit our community. We have a specific stack of constraints that rules out most of what's on a pharmacy shelf:

1. **Predominantly Black/dark skin** — most affordable sunscreens leave a white cast. Members will stop wearing them.
2. **Long outdoor swim sessions in Lagos UV** — UV index regularly hits 11–12. Daily-life SPF isn't enough.
3. **Water exposure for 60–90+ minutes** — only "water resistant 80 min" claims hold up in real swim use.
4. **Mixed ages 6–60+** — kids in academy need different products than adults.
5. **Price-sensitive in NGN** — must be restockable in Lagos at a member-realistic price.
6. **Chlorine-stripped skin barrier** — already compromised by pool exposure (per Dr. Adaeze's Q&A); harsh sunscreen filters make it worse.

The dermatology content series already covers chlorine + barrier repair. This guide closes the loop on **what to put on the skin before getting in the water**.

---

## Selection criteria

Any product we recommend or stock must hit all six:

| Criterion | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Broad spectrum, SPF 50+** | Lagos UV; UVA causes hyperpigmentation that disproportionately affects melanin-rich skin |
| **Water resistant 80 min** | Legal max claim; matches our typical session length |
| **No white cast** | Non-negotiable for dark skin; cast = members won't wear it |
| **Modern photostable filters** | Tinosorb S/M, Mexoryl 400, Uvinul A Plus — not old-generation oxybenzone |
| **Won't sting eyes** | Critical for swimmers; rules out alcohol-heavy fluids |
| **Available/restockable in Lagos** | Pharmacies, Jumia, or our own import — not a one-off member finds abroad |

## What we deliberately don't carry, and why

- **Banana Boat / classic Nivea Sun white cream** — heavy white cast on dark skin.
- **Black Girl Sunscreen SPF 30** — loved by the brand, but only SPF 30 and not water-resistance rated. Daily-life pick, not a swim pick.
- **Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun** — popular Korean option, but **not officially water-resistant**. Members swimming will reapply too often.
- **Spray sunscreens (Garnier, Nivea sprays)** — patchy coverage, especially on body hair and edges; bad value per gram of actual coverage.
- **Anything with oxybenzone or octinoxate** — older filters; pools recirculate, lagoons absorb, and these are linked to barrier irritation.

## How to use (member-facing protocol)

Adapt this for the on-site product page and the dermatology content series.

1. **Apply 15–20 minutes before getting in the water.** Sunscreen needs time to bind to the skin. Slap-and-jump means most washes off.
2. **Use enough.** A teaspoon for the face, a shot-glass for the body. Most people use a third of what they should and get a third of the SPF.
3. **Reapply every 80 minutes** of water exposure, or after toweling off. Water resistance ≠ waterproof.
4. **Don't forget:** ears, back of the neck, the part of your hairline, tops of feet, back of hands. These are the high-burn zones for swimmers.
5. **Pair with post-swim barrier care** (per Dr. Adaeze's "Rinse, Restore, Repair" protocol — see [SKINCARE_CONTENT_SERIES.md](../marketing/SKINCARE_CONTENT_SERIES.md)).

## Sourcing notes (internal)

### Manufacturer reality

**L'Oréal SA (France)** — owns LRP, Vichy, CeraVe, SkinCeuticals.
- **Mexoryl 400** is L'Oréal-proprietary. UVMune 400 line cannot be sourced from any other manufacturer at lower cost.
- Vichy Capital Soleil UV Age Daily uses the same filter — effectively a value sibling to LRP UVMune 400.
- L'Oréal Nigeria handles Active Cosmetics distribution; B2B requires registered business + NAFDAC compliance + minimum order commitment. Authorized reseller margin: 25–35% off RRP.

**Beiersdorf (Germany)** — owns Nivea, Eucerin.
- Stronger Africa distribution than L'Oréal. Easier B2B path for Eucerin SKUs.
- Uses Tinosorb S + M (BASF, openly licensable filters).

**Kao Corporation (Japan)** — makes Biore.
- Sourced via Asian importers in Lagos (Idumota network) or Dubai re-export. Lower unit cost, more legwork.

### Price tier strategy

- **Hero tier (LRP / Vichy hero body)** — premium, low-margin-but-credible, anchors the lineup.
- **Value tier (Eucerin)** — best margin opportunity once Beiersdorf B2B is in place. Likely the volume seller.
- **Budget tier (Biore)** — entry point. Members should leave the store with *something* they can afford, even on a tight month.

### Sourcing paths, ranked

1. **Pharmacy partnership (HealthPlus / Alpha / Medplus)** — fastest test of demand. Negotiate 10–15% member discount in exchange for promotion. Zero inventory risk. Recommended starting move.
2. **L'Oréal Nigeria & Beiersdorf Nigeria authorized B2B** — legitimate, slower onboarding (weeks), opens 25–35% margin. Worth pursuing once member demand is validated.
3. **Parallel imports from EU/UK** — ~40–50% landed cost reduction on paper, but: cosmetics duty ~20% + VAT 7.5%, NAFDAC registration (~₦200k+, weeks of paperwork), forex friction, parallel-import enforcement risk. Operationally heavy. Defer until volume justifies it.
4. **Dubai / UAE re-export** — middle ground, common path for Lagos beauty importers. Worth scoping once we know annual volume per SKU.

### Future option: SwimBuddz-branded white-label

The store moat isn't reselling LRP at margin — L'Oréal controls pricing too tightly. The defensible play is a **SwimBuddz-branded swim sunscreen** using **Tinosorb S + M** (BASF, openly licensable) via a Nigerian or South African contract manufacturer. Estimated COGS ₦3–5k, retail ₦8–12k. Stick format is travel-friendly and pool-bag-safe. Document this as a separate brief when capacity allows.

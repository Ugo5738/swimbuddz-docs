# SwimBuddz Pricing Strategy

*Last updated: June 2026*

## Core Principle: Every Tier Includes the One Below It

Don't stack fees. If someone has to pay community + club, the mental math becomes "I'm paying just to keep swimming after I already paid to learn." That feels like a trap. One price, one tier.

---

## Tier Structure

### Academy: N50,000/month (cohort-based)

- Duration depends on cohort (e.g., 12-week beginner cohort = N150,000)
- Includes **first month of club access** after graduation
- Frame it as: "Your academy membership keeps you swimming for 4 months — 3 months learning, 1 month practicing with your club group"
- The day after graduation, they already have a place to be next Saturday. No decision needed. No payment needed. Momentum carries.

### Club (includes community access)

- Quarterly: N42,500
- 6 months: N80,000
- 12 months: N150,000
- Kicks in after the free first month post-academy
- By then they've had 16 Saturdays in a row — paying to continue feels natural

Lead with the quarterly price. Only mention the longer commitments if someone asks about discounts — don't overwhelm new prospects with options. (And there is no standalone paid 1-month package — the only "1 month" in SwimBuddz is the free month that comes with academy graduation.)

### Community: N20,000/year

- Entry point for people who can already swim and just want access to the broader network, events, gear recommendations
- Also where club members land if they pause structured swimming but stay connected

## Academy Pricing by Segment

The ₦150k standard fee is the anchor — but price the academy cohort to the **pool and the segment**, not one-size-fits-all. Every price below is still all-inclusive (pool + coaching + first month of club):

- **Core (mainland / accessible): ₦120,000** — broad community, mainland partner pools. Maximises funnel entry; every graduate becomes a club/community member, which is the real lifetime value.
- **Standard: ₦150,000** — working professionals; the default.
- **Premium (Ikoyi/VI/Island, private or small-group): ₦180,000–220,000** — the affluent segment that already pays ~₦200k locally for ~10 private classes. Uses premium pools, smaller groups, or 1:1 (private cohort).
- **Corporate: ₦135,000 (5–9) / ₦127,500 (10+)** — volume justifies the discount. See [CORPORATE_WELLNESS.md](../marketing/CORPORATE_WELLNESS.md).

**Don't blanket-raise the standard to the Island number.** ₦200k there is *private/1:1* pricing, not group-cohort pricing — and as a young brand you don't yet have the testimonials to justify a ~3× premium over group competitors (Ocean28 ₦65k/12 sessions, Crawford ₦100k/8). **Raise into proof:** capture high willingness-to-pay through the *premium tier*, keep the core accessible to feed the community, and lift the standard price as transformation stories accumulate.

## Unit Economics & the Pool-Cost Rule

The academy fee is all-inclusive, so it must cover **pool + coach + margin**. These are the numbers behind every price above.

**Inputs — confirm before quoting (margins swing hard on these):**
- **Pool rate (confirmed — varies by location):** charged **per swimmer, per session** — mainland **₦3,000–5,000**, island **₦7,000–10,000**. Over 12 sessions: **₦36–60k/student mainland, ₦84–120k/student island.** Location is one of your biggest cost levers — see *Pool cost & capacity by location* below.
- **Sessions (confirmed):** **1 session/week (Saturdays) × 12 = 12 sessions.** (The ₦36k pool figure used below is the cheapest mainland rate, ₦3k × 12.)
- **Coach pay (confirmed — see [COACH_OPERATIONS_FRAMEWORK.md](../academy/COACH_OPERATIONS_FRAMEWORK.md) §6):** academy coaches earn a **revenue share by grade**, paid per 4-week block — Learn-to-Swim **Grade 1: 35–42%**, **Grade 2: 43–52%**. The standard **adult-beginner cohort is Grade 2 (~47%)**. **Corporate is different:** coaches are paid a **fixed fee per program** (negotiated), so corporate margin improves with team size.

**The rule, learned the hard way:** the pool fee is **per-swimmer**, so filling a cohort does **not** lower per-student pool cost (unlike a flat lane rental). Cost levers are only: the per-swimmer **rate** (i.e. location), the **session count**, and the **price**. **An island pool (₦7–10k/session = ₦84–120k/student) turns the standard ₦150k cohort into a loss** — so island cohorts must run on the premium tier (below), and standard/corporate pricing defaults to mainland pools.

**Academy margin per student** (12 sessions, ₦36k pool; revenue-share coach). Adult beginner = Grade 2, so the G2 column is your realistic default:

| Tier | Price | Pool | Coach G1 (40%) → net | Coach G2 (47%) → net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | ₦120,000 | ₦36,000 | ₦48,000 → **₦36,000** | ₦56,400 → **₦27,600** |
| Standard | ₦150,000 | ₦36,000 | ₦60,000 → **₦54,000** | ₦70,500 → **₦43,500** |
| Premium | ₦200,000 | ₦36,000 | ₦80,000 → **₦84,000** | ₦94,000 → **₦70,000** |

A Grade 2 standard cohort of 8 therefore nets **~₦348,000** per 12 weeks (₦43,500 × 8) after coach and pool. Lower-grade (simpler) programs net more; higher-grade (specialised) net less.

**Corporate is a different cost shape.** Corporate coaches are paid a **fixed fee per program**, not a revenue share, so each employee contributes **price − ₦36k pool** toward one fixed coach cost:
- ₦135,000 (5–9) → **₦99,000/employee** before the coach fee
- ₦127,500 (10+) → **₦91,500/employee** before the coach fee

So a 10-person corporate cohort throws off **~₦915,000** of contribution, minus one negotiated coach fee — i.e. corporate gets *more* profitable per head as the team grows. Set the fixed coach fee per contract.

**Format locked: 1 session/week (Saturdays), 12 sessions, ₦36k pool/student — the margins above are final, not illustrative.** (If you ever move to 2×/week, pool doubles to ₦72k and a Grade-2 standard cohort falls to ~₦7,500/student, so any future format change must be re-priced.)

### Pool cost & capacity by location

**Cost** is per-swimmer, per-session, and location-driven:

| Pool | Rate/session | Pool/student (×12) | Net/student at ₦150k (G2 coach) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mainland (e.g. Rowe Park) | ₦3,000–5,000 | ₦36,000–60,000 | **+₦43,500 to +₦19,500** ✓ |
| Island | ₦7,000–10,000 | ₦84,000–120,000 | **−₦4,500 to −₦40,500** ✗ |

So **standard and corporate cohorts default to a mainland pool; island pools only run on the premium tier (₦180k+)** — and even then a ₦10k island rate is tight with a revenue-share coach, so use a fixed-fee coach or price at ₦220k+. Saturday-morning timing is what makes asking island-based members to travel to a mainland pool reasonable (no weekday traffic).

**Capacity is the other half of the venue question — cheap isn't the same as suitable.** Rowe Park, for example, is a public pool: it opens 9am, fills fast from 10am, and we hold only 1–2 lanes. That caps what's possible for nervous beginners:
- **Right-size the group to the lanes** — plan ~4–6 beginners per lane/coach, not 10 in one space. A 10-person cohort needs 2 lanes, a second time-slot, or a bigger venue.
- **Take the 9–10am clear-water window** — get them in before the crowd; a packed public pool is the worst place to calm a frightened first-timer.
- **Larger corporate groups may need a bigger/more-exclusive pool**, which costs more — so the corporate/premium price has to carry it. Venue capacity, not just economics, caps cohort size.

## The Pipeline

```
Cold contact → Academy (N150k for 12 weeks, includes 1st month club) → Club (N42.5k/quarter) → Community (N20k/year if they pause)
```

Every tier includes the one below it. Nobody pays twice. Every transition has a bridge.

---

## The Critical Transition Moment

The transition from academy to club is where you'll lose people. Your cohort runs 12 weeks. Then 4-week gap. If during that gap you also ask them to pay again, most will say "let me take a break and think about it." They won't come back. The habit dies in that gap.

Including the first month of club in the academy fee solves this. They graduate on Saturday, and the next Saturday they're already part of a club group — no friction, no payment, no decision.

### The bridge does three things

- Removes payment friction at the most vulnerable moment
- Keeps the habit unbroken (16+ consecutive Saturdays)
- By the time the quarter payment hits, they're already embedded in the club community

---

## Founding Members: First Cohort Graduates

Consider making the first **quarter** of club free for your first 8 graduates, not just the first month. These people are your founding club members. They'll:

- Prove the model works
- Become your first Pod Leads
- Recruit the next wave through word of mouth

That's worth more than N340,000 in collected fees.

## Prospective Member Communication

When someone asks "what does registering in SwimBuddz entail?":

> SwimBuddz has 3 groups:
>
> **Community** — This is the home base for everyone regardless of swimming level. You get access to our events, open swim meetups, and the broader SwimBuddz network. Members look out for each other — you'll always pick up something new from fellow swimmers and coaches even at community sessions. N20k/year.
>
> **Club** — For people who want to swim together consistently every week. Structured Saturday sessions, group challenges, and a crew that holds you accountable. N42.5k/quarter.
>
> **Academy** — Structured cohorts where you actually learn — beginner programs, stroke development, competition prep depending on the level. A 12-week program is N150k.
>
> [Tailor the recommendation to their situation]

### Key principles for the pitch:
1. **Lead with what they get, not what they pay** — describe the experience first, then the cost
2. **Show one price per tier** — no decision fatigue for a first conversation
3. **Position community as a choice**, not a consolation prize
4. **Connect to what they've seen** or experienced if possible
5. **Tailor the close** — recommend based on their situation without making lesser tiers feel lesser

---

## Why This Works

- **No fee stacking** — each tier is one clean price
- **Bridge periods** eliminate the gap where habits die
- **Downgrade path exists** — community catches people who pause, keeping them in the ecosystem
- **Upgrade path is natural** — community members see club energy at events and want in
- **Academy grads don't have to make a decision** — they're already swimming with their club group before the invoice arrives
